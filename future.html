I want a simple, disciplined, and balanced life: my faith at the center, strong technical skills, honest work, and building systems that are used by many people.

Looking Ahead

Where do you see yourself in 5 to 10 years?

For the next five years, I want to keep working as a developer and get much stronger technically. I don’t want to move into a leadership role too early. After that, I want to step up as a tech lead — someone who guides technical decisions while still staying close to the code.

  • Years 1–5 — Developer: Grow into a senior developer with real depth in system design and architecture. Own features from start to finish, join an international team, and learn how good tech leads make decisions.
  • Years 5–10 — Tech Lead: Lead a small team, shape the technical direction of a product, and help other developers grow — while still writing code myself.
What kind of work do you want to be doing?
Work on products used by a large number of users, where performance, reliability, and good architecture really matter. I enjoy the full stack, but I’m especially drawn to frontend architecture, backend systems, and infrastructure.
Where do you want to work?
With an international team. I want to work with engineers from different countries and backgrounds, learn how strong teams around the world build software, and hold myself to that standard. In the longer term, I hope to work abroad — and I’m preparing for it by improving my English speaking and building the technical depth that global companies expect.

Personality

How would you describe yourself?
Analytical, curious, and practical. I like understanding how things work underneath instead of just following instructions. I prefer simple solutions over clever ones, and I’m most comfortable with straightforward, honest communication.
What values guide you?
My faith as a Muslim is the foundation. From there: simplicity, discipline, and integrity. I try to keep my commitments and be honest about what I know and don’t know.
What are you still working on?
Speaking English with confidence, and feeling more at ease in unfamiliar social settings. I’m naturally more comfortable with focused work and meaningful one-on-one conversations than large group events, so I’m deliberately practicing both.

Motivation

What motivates you?

Understanding things deeply. I enjoy the moment a complex system finally makes sense, and turning that understanding into something cleaner and easier to maintain.

Building at scale. I want to build systems that many people use every day. Scale brings interesting problems — performance, reliability, and architecture — and solving them well is what I find satisfying.

Getting a little better every year. Technically and personally. Steady progress matters more to me than quick wins.

Why do you want to stay technical before leading?
Because I believe a good tech lead needs real technical depth first. I’d rather earn that role by understanding systems well than step into it before I’m ready.

Working Preferences

What kind of environment helps you do your best work?
A collaborative team with supportive colleagues, reasonable engineering standards, and room to keep learning. I work best with clear goals and some autonomy in how to reach them.
How do you organize your work?
I like a consistent routine, focused blocks of time, and checklists. I try to minimize distractions, and when I see a repetitive task, my first instinct is to automate it.
How do you like to communicate with your team?
Directly and honestly. I’d rather hear a clear “this won’t work, here’s why” than polite vagueness, and I try to give others the same. I also like explaining the reasoning behind decisions, not just the decision itself.
What kind of tech lead do you want to become?
One who still codes with the team, keeps systems simple, makes decisions based on reasoning rather than opinion, and gives honest feedback in a respectful way.

Growth

What do you want to learn in the coming years?
  • System design and software architecture.
  • AI agents and automation — using them to reduce repetitive work and build more with less.
  • Infrastructure, scalability, and Linux.
  • Speaking English confidently in meetings and discussions — the key step toward working abroad.
  • Technical leadership: making trade-offs, writing clear proposals, and guiding others.

Life Beyond Work

What does a balanced life look like for you?
Simple and structured. I give my full focus during work hours, and when work is done, I keep my personal time for rest, prayer, and reading the Qur’an. I don’t believe in working all the time — a clear boundary between work and life keeps me consistent for the long run.
What do you hope will never change about you?
Tools and job titles will change. I hope I’ll always stay curious, keep my life simple, stay honest in my work, and keep my faith at the center of everything I do.
